# Certificate Attestation in Delhi: How to Plan for Different Document Types

When several documents are needed for an overseas application, it can be tempting to submit them together and expect the same authentication process for every certificate. In practice, the issuing authority, document category, destination country and purpose of use can affect the steps required.

A university degree needed for employment, a birth certificate required for a family application and a commercial document prepared for an international transaction may require different preliminary checks.

Instead of treating attestation as a single administrative task, applicants should approach it as a document-planning exercise. The objective is to identify the correct requirements for each record before submitting original documents or paying for services.

## 1. Build a Document Plan Before Starting

Begin by creating a list of every document required by the receiving organisation. This is particularly useful when preparing paperwork for overseas employment, higher education, immigration, family matters or business activities.

For each document, record four details:

- **Document type:** Degree certificate, birth certificate, marriage certificate, affidavit, commercial record or another document.
- **Issuing authority:** The university, educational board, government department, notary or other organisation responsible for issuing it.
- **Destination:** The country where the document will be presented.
- **Purpose:** Employment, admission, family sponsorship, legal proceedings, business registration or another use.

This information creates a clearer picture of what needs to be verified. It also helps identify documents that may require separate preliminary authentication.

For example, a person applying for an overseas job might need a degree certificate and a marriage certificate for different administrative purposes. Although both documents are part of one application, their authentication requirements may not be identical.

## 2. Why the Issuing Authority Matters

The place where a document was issued can be as important as the place where the applicant currently lives.

Delhi-based applicants may hold certificates issued by universities, educational boards, government departments or institutions in other states. The required preliminary authentication can depend on the issuing authority and the applicable procedure.

This means that living in Delhi does not automatically establish that every document can begin at the same office or follow an identical verification route.

Before applying, confirm:

- Which authority is responsible for preliminary authentication.
- Whether the issuing institution must verify the document first.
- Whether the relevant authority offers a supported digital verification route.
- Which documents must be submitted for the next stage.
- Whether the destination country requires apostille or normal attestation.

The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) publishes the applicable attestation and apostille guidance at https://www.mea.gov.in/apostille-menu.htm.

Checking these details early can be particularly useful when an application contains documents issued by multiple institutions.

## 3. Educational Certificates: Plan Around Academic Records

Educational documents are frequently required for overseas study, employment and professional registration. However, applicants should not assume that all academic records are treated identically.

A document list may include a degree certificate, diploma, school certificate, transcript or marksheet. The receiving institution may require one or several of these records.

Before arranging certificate attestation in Delhi, check the following:

**Confirm the exact academic documents required.** A university or employer may request a degree certificate, academic transcript or additional records. Verify the list rather than preparing only the document you consider most important.

**Identify the issuing institution.** The verification route may depend on the university, board or other authority that issued the record.

**Check the receiving organisation's rules.** Some institutions specify document formats, translation requirements or particular authentication procedures.

**Keep related records organised.** If several academic documents are required, maintain a separate checklist for each one and confirm whether each needs its own authentication.

These checks can help applicants avoid discovering missing requirements after the process has already started.

## 4. Personal Documents: Check the Legal Purpose

Personal documents can include birth certificates, marriage certificates, affidavits, powers of attorney and other records used for official or legal purposes.

Their requirements may differ because they are created and issued through different processes. A government-issued civil certificate, for instance, is not the same as a privately prepared affidavit.

Applicants should establish the document's legal purpose before choosing an authentication route. A birth certificate required for a family application may have different supporting-document requirements from a power of attorney intended for use in a legal transaction abroad.

Check whether the receiving authority requires the original document, a particular certificate format, translation or any preliminary authentication. If a name or date differs across supporting records, ask the relevant authority how the discrepancy should be addressed rather than altering the certificate yourself.

## 5. Commercial Documents Need a Separate Plan

Businesses preparing documents for international use should avoid treating commercial paperwork as a routine extension of personal certificate attestation.

Depending on the transaction, documents may include company records, agreements, invoices, certificates of origin or other trade-related paperwork. Some commercial documents require preliminary verification by a chamber of commerce or another competent authority.

Before arranging authentication, businesses should identify:

- The organisation requesting the document.
- The exact purpose of the overseas transaction.
- The authority that issued or certified the record.
- The preliminary verification required.
- The destination country's authentication rules.

If a company has several documents, create a separate checklist for each document category. This makes it easier to spot records that require different verification steps and reduces the risk of applying the same procedure indiscriminately.

## 6. Apostille or Normal Attestation: Make the Right Choice

One of the most important decisions is determining which authentication route the destination country requires.

An **apostille** is used for eligible public documents under the Hague Apostille Convention. Normal attestation is relevant where apostille is not accepted and the applicable procedure requires another form of authentication or legalisation.

The correct route should be confirmed before submission. Do not assume that apostille is always the faster option or that it is suitable for every destination.

The MEA explains the distinction between apostille and normal attestation in its official guidance: https://www.mea.gov.in/apostille-menu.htm.

Applicants can also check the government's e-Sanad platform to see whether their document and issuing authority are supported for online verification and authentication. Availability depends on the relevant digital records and applicable requirements.

Official portal: https://esanad.nic.in/

An apostille or attestation authenticates a document's origin according to the applicable procedure. It does not guarantee that a university, employer, licensing body or immigration authority will accept the document for every purpose.

## 7. How to Organise a Multi-Document Application

If several records need authentication, organise them before approaching a service provider.

A simple planning sheet can include:

| Detail | What to record |
|---|---|
| Document | Exact name of each certificate or record |
| Issuing authority | Institution or department responsible for issuing it |
| Destination | Country where it will be used |
| Purpose | Employment, education, family, legal or commercial use |
| Preliminary checks | Verification or certification required before submission |
| Final route | Apostille or normal attestation, as applicable |
| Status | Pending, submitted, awaiting clarification or completed |

This approach helps applicants see which documents are ready and which still require attention. It is also useful when several family members or employees have documents that must be prepared for the same destination.

Keep copies of submitted records and application references. Confirm the current submission procedure, fees and expected timelines before making commitments based on a particular completion date.
